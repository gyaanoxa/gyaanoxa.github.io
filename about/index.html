About

About Gyaanoxa

A Hindi channel about the parts of Indian history that textbooks skip: kings and wars, festivals, scientists and a few old mysteries. Told in the Hindi we speak at home, with the sources named.

Our story

Why Gyaanoxa exists

Most of us learned history as a list of dates. The real stories, like a prince who locked up his own father or an emperor who carved his regret in stone, were left out. Gyaanoxa tells those stories the way they were told at home: simply, in Hindi, one story at a time.

The name comes from ज्ञान (knowledge). Every story stays close to its sources, so you can enjoy it and still trust it.

How we work

How a story is made

1. Pick the story

Topics come from viewers' questions, festival dates and the stories that fit the timeline next.

2. Read the sources

History books, inscriptions and encyclopaedias. Where two old texts disagree, both versions are noted.

3. Write in simple Hindi

One clear story for a one minute Short, and a longer version for the full video and the article.

4. Pictures with credit

Old paintings and photos are credited by name and licence. Drawn scenes only explain what words cannot.

5. Publish everywhere

YouTube, Instagram and Facebook at 7 PM IST, then the long article goes up on this website.

6. Listen and fix

Comments and emails decide the next topics, and every correction with a source is applied.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is Gyaanoxa?

Gyaanoxa (ज्ञानोक्सा) is a Hindi history channel. It tells the parts of Indian history that school books often skip: kings and wars, the old stories behind festivals, scientists and a few mysteries.

When do new stories come out?

A new Short goes live every evening at 7 PM IST. A big story first comes out part by part as Shorts, and the full video follows at 6 PM after the last part. Festival stories come out at 1 PM a day before the festival.

Where does the information come from?

From history books, inscriptions and encyclopaedias, mostly Wikipedia and the sources it cites. Every video description names its source. Legends are told as legends, for example "the old books say".

Whose pictures are in the videos?

Old paintings and photos come from places like Wikimedia Commons, and each artist and licence is listed in the video description. Some scenes are drawn only to explain the story, and they are not real photos.

Where can I watch?

On YouTube (@Gyaanoxa), Instagram (@gyaanoxa) and Facebook (facebook.com/gyaanoxa). The long version of every story is on this website's Blog.

I found a mistake. What should I do?

Write to gyaanoxa@gmail.com with the source. A mistake reported with a source is fixed in the article and in the next video.

Can I suggest a topic?

Yes. Many stories on the channel started as a viewer's question. Send the name of the king, battle, festival or mystery, and where you read about it.
