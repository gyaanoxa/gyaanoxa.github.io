    What the sources say

    Facts & sources

    • Birsa Munda was born in Ulihatu (now in Khunti district, Jharkhand). The date is disputed: most accounts give 15 November 1875, while some sources say 18 July 1872. Folk songs name either Ulihatu or Chalkad as his birthplace.
    • He was a poor Munda boy who grazed sheep and was an expert flute player. He studied at Salga under Jaipal Nag, converted to Christianity to join the German Mission School, and was renamed Birsa David, which later became Birsa Daud.
    • He lived at Chaibasa from 1886 to 1890. His father Sugana Munda withdrew him from school. In 1890 the family gave up the German mission and returned to their traditional tribal religion.
    • The forest rules of 1893-94 and the long loss of tribal land to outside farmers and Thikadars drew him into protest. He joined the forest agitation led by Gideon of Piring.
    • In 1895 he is said to have seen a vision of a supreme God. He became known as a preacher, healer and miracle-worker, and was called Dharti Aaba. He told farmers to pay no rent and was arrested on 24 August 1895. He was jailed for two years and released on 28 January 1898.
    • Around Christmas 1899, about 7,000 people are said to have launched the Ulgulan. Missions, police stations and constables were attacked. He allegedly promised that guns and bullets would turn to water. Police fired at Sail Rakab Hill on 9 January 1900, and the death toll is unknown.
    • With a Rs 500 reward on his head, he was arrested in the Jamkopai forest on 3 February 1900, and the news reached London. 460 tribals were accused in 15 cases, 63 were convicted, and 6 people died in prison, including Birsa, who died on 9 June 1900.
    • The movement faded after his death. In 1908 the colonial government brought in the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act, which bars the transfer of tribal land to non-tribals.

    Source: Birsa Munda (Wikipedia) and the texts it cites. Legends are told as legends.
