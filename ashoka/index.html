What the sources say

Facts & sources

  • Ashoka (c. 304-232 BCE) was the third Maurya emperor and ruled from c. 268 BCE from Pataliputra. His empire stretched from present-day Afghanistan to present-day Bangladesh.
  • According to the Ashokavadana, his father Bindusara disliked him for his rough skin. Even so, an ascetic saw that Ashoka would be the next king. Legends disagree on where he was sent: the North Indian tradition says Takshashila, the Sri Lankan tradition says Ujjain.
  • Legends say he took the throne by killing his brothers: 99 in the Mahavamsa and 100 in the Dipavamsa, while the Ashokavadana says Susima was tricked into a charcoal pit. Historians think these numbers are exaggerated, though he may have killed one or several brothers.
  • The Ashokavadana calls him 'Chandashoka' and describes 500 ministers beheaded and a torture prison called 'Ashoka's Hell'. Scholars see these stories as exaggerations by Buddhist authors who wanted his conversion to look like a miracle.
  • In his 8th regnal year (c. 260 BCE) he conquered Kalinga. Major Rock Edict 13 records 100,000 killed and 150,000 captured, and expresses deep remorse. However, this edict is missing in the Kalinga region itself.
  • Accounts of his conversion conflict. The Sri Lankan tradition says the monk Nigrodha, the son of a brother Ashoka had killed, converted him before Kalinga. His own edicts suggest a gradual change in the years after the war.
  • Legends credit him with 84,000 stupas or viharas. His dhamma pillars and edicts promoted the end of the death penalty, medical care for humans and animals, and respect for all faiths. The Sri Lankan tradition says his children Mahinda and Sanghamitta went to Sri Lanka, but historians debate this.
  • The Ashokavadana also claims he committed violence after his conversion, but scholars call these stories sectarian fabrications. The Brahmi script was later forgotten until James Prinsep studied it in the 19th century. Today India's State Emblem and the Ashoka Chakra come from his legacy.

Source: Ashoka (Wikipedia) and the texts it cites. Legends are told as legends.

Quiz

How much do you remember?

5 questions. Every answer is in this story.

1. शिलालेख 13 के अनुसार कलिंग युद्ध में कितने लोग मारे गए?

शिलालेख 13 में 1 लाख मौतें और 1.5 लाख बंदी लिखे हैं।

2. किताबों के अनुसार अशोक ने गद्दी के लिए कितने भाइयों को मारा?

महावंश 99 और दीपवंश 100 कहता है, इतिहासकार इन संख्याओं को बढ़ा-चढ़ाकर लिखा मानते हैं।

3. कलिंग युद्ध वाला शिलालेख 13 कहाँ नहीं मिला?

पछतावे वाला यह शिलालेख खुद कलिंग इलाके में नहीं है।

4. अशोक के समय की ब्राह्मी लिपि को 19वीं सदी में किसने फिर पढ़ा?

ब्राह्मी लिपि भुला दी गई थी; जेम्स प्रिंसेप ने उसे फिर पढ़ा।

5. किंवदंतियाँ अशोक को कितने स्तूप या विहार बनाने का श्रेय देती हैं?

परंपरा 84,000 स्तूप/विहार बताती है।
