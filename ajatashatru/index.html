What the sources say

Facts & sources

  • Ajatashatru (called Kunika or Kuniya in Jain tradition) was the son of King Bimbisara, one of the most important kings of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha, and a contemporary of both Mahavira and Gautama Buddha; his reign is dated either c. 492 to 460 BCE or c. 405 to 373 BCE.
  • He took the kingdom of Magadha from his father by force and imprisoned him.
  • Both traditions tell a similar birth legend. In the Jain version the pregnant queen craved the fried flesh of her husband's heart; in the Buddhist version she wanted to drink blood from Bimbisara's arm. The baby was thrown near a garbage dump, and Bimbisara sucked his wounded little finger until it healed, which gave him the Jain name Kunika, 'Sore Finger'.
  • Jain account of the Vaishali war: Queen Padmavati wanted the elephant Sechanaka and the 18-fold divine necklace owned by his brothers Halla and Vihalla. The brothers fled to their maternal grandfather Chetaka of Vaishali. In total 570000 infantrymen fought. Chetaka shot one arrow a day and killed the 10 Kalakumaras. With the help of the Indras, Ajatashatru used Mahasilakantaka (more than 100,000 died) and the self-moving spiked chariot Ratha-Musala. The monk Kulvalaka was tricked into getting the city's protecting chaitya uprooted, and Vaishali fell.
  • Buddhist account: the dispute was over a diamond mine on the Ganges whose diamonds the Licchavis mostly carried away. Chief minister Vassakara split the Vajjis, and a scythed chariot with a swinging mace and blades was used to conquer the town.
  • The two traditions describe a different Ajatashatru. Jain texts name his mother Chellana (daughter of Chetaka) and give him 8 wives. Buddhist texts name his mother Kosala Devi and give him 500 wives, with Vajira as chief consort. The Buddhist texts also say that after Bimbisara's murder, Prasenajit took back Kashi, the dowry city, and the resulting war ended in a treaty and the marriage to Vajira.
  • Magadha under Ajatashatru became the most powerful kingdom in North India, and he turned the village of Pataliputra into a fortified city.
  • Buddhist texts say he visited six teachers and finally the Buddha. He realized his mistakes, his association with Devadatta and his plan to kill his father, and took refuge in the Buddha, Dhamma and Sangha. He built a stupa on the Buddha's relics and attended the first Buddhist council. Jain texts say he held Mahavira in the highest esteem.

Source: Ajatashatru (Wikipedia) and the texts it cites. Legends are told as legends.

Quiz

कितना याद रहा?

5 सवाल — हर जवाब इसी कहानी से।

1. अजातशत्रु के पिता कौन थे?

वह हर्यंक वंश के राजा बिम्बिसार का बेटा था।

2. जैन परंपरा में अजातशत्रु का नाम 'कुणिक' क्यों पड़ा?

'कुणिक' का अर्थ है घायल उंगली — पिता ने उसे चूसकर ठीक किया था।

3. जैन ग्रंथों के अनुसार वैशाली युद्ध किस चीज़ के लिए हुआ?

रानी पद्मावती हाथी सेचनक और 18 लड़ियों वाला दिव्य हार चाहती थीं। बौद्ध ग्रंथ हीरे की खान बताते हैं।

4. बौद्ध ग्रंथों के अनुसार अजातशत्रु की कितनी पत्नियाँ थीं?

बौद्ध ग्रंथ 500 पत्नियाँ बताते हैं, जैन ग्रंथ 8।

5. किस गाँव को अजातशत्रु ने किलेबंद नगर बनाया, जो आगे चलकर राजधानी बना?

पाटलिपुत्र (आज का पटना) बाद में मौर्यों की राजधानी बना।
