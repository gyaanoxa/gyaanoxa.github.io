    What the sources say

    Facts & sources

    • Bindusara (r. c. 297, c. 273 BCE) was the second Mauryan emperor, son of Chandragupta and father of Ashoka; most of what we know about him comes from legends written centuries after his death.
    • Name legend: Jain and Buddhist texts say Chanakya fed Chandragupta small doses of poison, and the pregnant queen ate some by mistake. In the Buddhist version the baby was kept for seven days in the bellies of freshly killed goats and was named for the goat-blood spots (bindu). In Hemachandra's Jain version, a drop of poison touched the baby's head, so the name means 'strength of the drop'. The queen's name there is Durdhara.
    • Greek writers called him Amitrochates or Allitrochades, probably from the Sanskrit title Amitraghata, 'slayer of enemies'. He was also called Devanampriya, and one Jain text says his birth name was Simhasena.
    • Taranatha (16th century) says Chanakya destroyed the nobles and kings of 16 towns and made Bindusara master of the land between the western and eastern seas. Historians disagree on whether this means conquest of the Deccan, crushing revolts, or just holding what he inherited.
    • Hemachandra's legend: the minister Subandhu, out of jealousy, told Bindusara that Chanakya had cut open his mother's belly. Bindusara turned against Chanakya, then regretted it. Subandhu pretended to make peace with Chanakya and burned him to death, and Chanakya's curse later forced Subandhu to become a monk.
    • Bindusara was on friendly terms with the Greeks. Athenaeus, quoting Hegesander, says he asked Antiochus I for sweet wine, dried figs and a sophist. Antiochus agreed to the wine and figs but said Greek law forbade selling a sophist.
    • In the Ashokavadana, Bindusara favoured Sushima and disliked Ashoka. Minister Khallataka and the 500 councillors backed Ashoka. While Sushima was failing to crush a revolt in Takshashila, the angry, dying king saw the crown placed on Ashoka's head, and Radhagupta later tricked Sushima into a pit of burning charcoal.
    • Other sources disagree: the Brahmanda Purana says Bindusara crowned Ashoka himself, and the Rajavali-Katha says he retired after handing Ashoka the throne.

    Source: Bindusara (Wikipedia) and the texts it cites. Legends are told as legends.
